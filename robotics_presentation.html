System Modeling

Translational Mechanical Elements ($M, K, f$)

(1) Mass ($M$)
Kinetic Storage

Rigid body inertia following Newton's 2nd Law.

$$F(t) = M \frac{d^2x}{dt^2} = M \ddot{x}$$

Energy: $E_k = \frac{1}{2} M v^2$

(2) Spring ($K$)
Potential Storage

Linear elasticity following Hooke's Law.

$$F(t) = K (x_1 - x_2)$$

Energy: $E_p = \frac{1}{2} K (\Delta x)^2$

(3) Damper ($f$)
Energy Dissipator

Viscous friction drag proportional to relative velocity.

$$F(t) = f (\dot{x}_1 - \dot{x}_2)$$

Power Loss: $P = f (\Delta v)^2$

System Modeling

Rotational Mechanical Elements ($J, K, f$)

(1) Moment of Inertia ($J$)
Rotational Kinetic

Rotational mass inertia resisting angular acceleration.

$$T = J \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = J \ddot{\theta}$$

Units: $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$

(2) Torsional Spring ($K$)
Rotational Potential

Shaft torsional flexibility under torque.

$$T = K (\theta_1 - \theta_2)$$

Units: $\text{N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$

(3) Rotational Damper ($f$)
Viscous Drag

Bearing viscous fluid resistance.

$$T = f (\dot{\theta}_1 - \dot{\theta}_2)$$

Units: $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s/rad}$

Worked Derivation

Parallel Spring-Mass-Damper Differential Equation

System Setup & Free Body Diagram

Mass $M$ attached in parallel to elastic spring $K$ and viscous damper $f$, driven by external force $F(t)$.

$$\mathbf{M \frac{d^2x(t)}{dt^2} + f \frac{dx(t)}{dt} + K x(t) = F(t)}$$
Universal 4-Step Protocol
  1. Define Coordinates: Displacement $x(t)$.
  2. Draw FBD: Isolate mass $M$ with spring force $Kx$ and damping force $f\dot{x}$.
  3. Apply Newton's 2nd Law: $\sum F = M \ddot{x}$.
  4. Arrange ODE Form: Second-order linear differential equation.

Analytical Derivation

Rotating Disc Dynamics & Laplace Solution

System ODE ($J, f, K$)
$$0.2 \ddot{\theta}(t) + 0.5 \dot{\theta}(t) + 5 \theta(t) = 2 \sin(3t)$$

Transforming to Laplace domain with zero initial conditions:

$$\Theta(s) = \frac{6}{(s^2 + 9)(0.2 s^2 + 0.5 s + 5)}$$
Final Time-Domain $\theta(t)$
$$\mathbf{\theta(t) = -0.2401 \cos(3t) + 0.51 \sin(3t) + e^{-1.25 t} \left[ 0.2401 \cos(4.84 t) - 0.125 \sin(4.84 t) \right]}$$

Combines steady-state sinusoidal response with transient exponential decay.

Dynamic System Balance
A cart trolley of mass $M$ (displacement $x_1$) drives a pivoted manipulator arm of mass $m$, length $a$, and moment of inertia $J$ (angle $\theta$).
1. Cart Motion Balance
$$\mathbf{(M + m) \ddot{x}_1 + m a \cos\theta \, \ddot{\theta} - m a \sin\theta \, \dot{\theta}^2 = F(t)}$$
2. Arm Rotational Balance
$$\mathbf{(J + m a^2) \ddot{\theta} + m a \cos\theta \, \ddot{x}_1 + m a g \sin\theta = T(t)}$$

D-H Convention

Denavit-Hartenberg (D-H) 4-Parameter Convention

1. Joint Angle ($\theta_i$)

Rotation about $\hat{Z}_{i-1}$ axis to align $\hat{X}_{i-1}$ with $\hat{X}_i$. (Variable for Revolute Joint).

2. Joint Offset ($d_i$)

Translation along $\hat{Z}_{i-1}$ axis to common normal. (Variable for Prismatic Joint).

3. Link Length ($a_i$)

Distance along $\hat{X}_i$ axis connecting $\hat{Z}_{i-1}$ and $\hat{Z}_i$.

4. Link Twist ($\alpha_i$)

Angle about $\hat{X}_i$ axis to align $\hat{Z}_{i-1}$ with $\hat{Z}_i$.

$$\mathbf{T_{i-1}^i = T_{Rz}(\theta_i) \cdot T_z(d_i) \cdot T_x(a_i) \cdot T_{Rx}(\alpha_i)}$$

Numerical Optimization

Inverse Kinematics via Gradient Descent Algorithm

Gradient Descent Update Rule
$$\mathbf{\theta^{(k+1)} = \theta^{(k)} + \alpha \, J(\theta^{(k)})^T \cdot \left( X_d - f(\theta^{(k)}) \right)}$$

Iteratively updates joint angle vector $\theta$ by scaling transposed Jacobian $J^T$ against cartesian error vector $\Delta X$.

Trajectory Generation

3rd-Order Cubic Polynomial Joint Trajectories

Cubic Position & Velocity Polynomials
$$q(t) = a_0 + a_1 t + a_2 t^2 + a_3 t^3$$
$$\dot{q}(t) = a_1 + 2 a_2 t + 3 a_3 t^2$$

Minimum polynomial degree to satisfy 4 boundary conditions ($q(0)=\theta_0, q(t_f)=\theta_f, \dot{q}(0)=0, \dot{q}(t_f)=0$).

Closed-Form Coefficient Formulas
$$a_0 = \theta_0, \qquad a_1 = 0$$
$$\mathbf{a_2 = \frac{3(\theta_f - \theta_0)}{t_f^2}}, \qquad \mathbf{a_3 = -\frac{2(\theta_f - \theta_0)}{t_f^3}}$$

Dynamic Modeling

Kinematic Model vs. Dynamic Model Comparison

Feature Kinematic Model Dynamic Model
1. Motion BasisDescribes motion without forces or torques.Describes motion due to applied forces & torques.
2. System InputsJoint angles ($\theta$) and velocities ($\dot{\theta}$).Motor torques ($\tau$) and external forces ($F_{\text{ext}}$).
3. System OutputsEnd-effector position ($P$) and orientation ($R$).Joint accelerations ($\ddot{\theta}$) and dynamic response.
4. FoundationBased on system geometry (D-H matrices).Based on Newton's laws / Euler-Lagrange mechanics.
5. ApplicationTrajectory / path planning.Closed-loop control design & torque computation.
